    Does it work across skin tones?

    Stage 1 (normal vs rash) on held-out real smartphone photos, by Fitzpatrick skin type. At one shared threshold the model missed more rashes on darker skin, even though it ranked them almost as well (similar AUC). Choosing your skin type applies a calibrated threshold.

    Built to support your dermatologist, not replace them

    A dermatologist sees your skin for a few minutes every few weeks. Eczema flares build over days in between. DermEdge records those days, so your doctor sees the trend, not a snapshot.

    Consistent

    It scores the same photo the same way every time, so a change in the score is a change in your skin.

    Tracks between visits

    Daily check-ins per body site, alongside your treatments, journal and wearable triggers.

    Measurable change

    "I think it's better" becomes "inner elbow 6.1 → 4.3", something you and your doctor can act on.

    Explains itself

    The heatmap shows where the model looked, so your doctor can check its reasoning.

    How accurate is this?

    What we measured, and what we haven't yet. DermEdge is a screening aid: a dermatologist makes the diagnosis.

    • The rash screen misses about 1 in 6 rashes. It caught 1,182 of 1,444 rashes on real phone photos (SCIN), tested on people it never saw in training.
    • Eczema vs look-alikes is right about 4 times in 5. 81% accuracy on 507 clinical photos. Accuracy on phone and webcam photos hasn't been measured yet.
    • Severity tends to rate mild eczema as moderate. Off by 1.2 points on average on the 0–12 scale, and within one band 98% of the time.
    • Darker skin needs your skin type to be set. On Fitzpatrick V–VI the screen caught 67% of rashes at the default setting and 83% once calibrated. See the Doctor report tab.
    • Flare risk is a designed score, not yet clinically validated. It multiplies the photo result by your triggers. Checking it against real flares needs a study over time.
    • The wristband is simulated in this browser version. The hardware firmware is built but hasn't been tested on real boards yet.
